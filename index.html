About this piece

A slow, ambient tour of the chain of physics behind space weather: the Sun's activity, the solar wind it blows across the solar system, Earth's magnetic shield, and the aurora where that energy lands in our upper atmosphere.

Measured data drives every scene: the Kp and Ap geomagnetic indices, F10.7 solar radio flux, sunspot number, and the solar wind speed, density, and magnetic field direction (Bz) measured upstream of Earth at the L1 point.

Everything you see is a visualization, not an image. Shapes and colors are procedural and stylized after how scientists display these phenomena (for example SDO/AIA 304 Å false color, IMAGE and Polar ultraviolet auroral images). Sizes and distances are not to scale. Where a number comes from a physics model rather than a measurement, it is labeled "model".

Sound

The Orchestra is the Space Weather Sonifier built in: each data set is a player with its own voice (airport piano, slow pad, shimmer glass, soft choir, bell, glass, pluck), on a long reverb and a tempo-synced delay. One step is one 3-hour Kp reading. Every note's pitch becomes a color (hue = pitch class × 30°, C = red), and that color flashes in the scene where the data lives: F10.7 and sunspots on the Sun, wind speed and density along the Parker spiral, Bz on the magnetopause, Kp and Ap in the aurora. Sound starts only when you press Sound or Play.

Data sources

  • NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center (SWPC): planetary K-index, real-time solar wind and IMF, 10.7 cm flux, SWPC sunspot number. Fetched live when your browser can reach it.
  • GFZ Helmholtz Centre for Geosciences, Potsdam: Kp history, Ap, F10.7 observed, international sunspot number (SILSO). Included as a bundled snapshot.

Controls

Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom. Keys: ← → change scene, T tour, R replay, S sound (play/pause the orchestra), O orchestra panel, B Bartels strip, H hide text, I about.
