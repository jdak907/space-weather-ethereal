About this piece

Created by Jeff Dumps · github.com/jdak907

A slow, ambient tour of the chain of physics behind space weather: the Sun's activity, the solar wind it blows across the solar system, Earth's magnetic shield, the aurora where that energy lands in our upper atmosphere, and the Schumann resonances: the whole planet's electromagnetic ringing, driven by lightning.

Measured data drives every scene: the Kp and Ap geomagnetic indices, F10.7 solar radio flux, sunspot number, and the solar wind speed, density, and magnetic field direction (Bz) measured upstream of Earth at the L1 point.

One timeline. Every data set sits on one UTC clock as experienced at Earth. Solar wind measured at the L1 point is shifted to when it reaches Earth (1.41 million km ÷ its measured speed, about 30 to 60 minutes). The Sun scene shows the Sun 8.3 minutes earlier, when the light left it. When the playhead passes a flare, proton event, southward Bz, or storm, the scenes show the cause first and then each effect after its physical delay, and the tour follows the chain.

Schumann resonance is modeled. No open, live, numeric Schumann feed exists for web pages (stations publish pictures). Its intensity comes from where tropical land storms are in the afternoon for each UTC hour. Its frequencies shift with live GOES X-rays (up about 0.2 Hz for big flares) and proton events (down 0.15 to 0.4 Hz).

Everything you see is a visualization, not an image. Shapes and colors are procedural and stylized after how scientists display these phenomena (for example SDO/AIA 304 Å false color, IMAGE and Polar ultraviolet auroral images). Sizes and distances are not to scale. Where a number comes from a physics model rather than a measurement, it is labeled "model".

Sound

The Orchestra is the Space Weather Sonifier built in: each data set (now including GOES X-rays and the modeled Schumann resonance) is a player with its own voice (airport piano, slow pad, shimmer glass, soft choir, bell, glass, pluck, and a cavity drone for the Schumann modes), on a long reverb and a tempo-synced delay. All sets share one UTC timeline; one step is 45 minutes of real time by default. Every note's pitch becomes a color (hue = pitch class × 30°, C = red), and that color flashes in the scene where the data lives: F10.7 and sunspots on the Sun, wind speed and density along the Parker spiral, Bz on the magnetopause, Kp and Ap in the aurora. Sound starts only when you press Sound or Play.

Data sources

  • NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center (SWPC): planetary K-index, real-time solar wind and IMF, 10.7 cm flux, SWPC sunspot number. Fetched live when your browser can reach it.
  • NOAA SWPC GOES X-ray flux and ≥10 MeV protons (live, with a bundled 7-day snapshot).
  • Natural Earth 1:110m land (public domain) for the continents in the Schumann scene.
  • GFZ Helmholtz Centre for Geosciences, Potsdam: Kp history, Ap, F10.7 observed, international sunspot number (SILSO). Included as a bundled snapshot.

Times

Every time is shown three ways: your local time (with its zone), UTC, and Greenwich Mean Sidereal Time (GMST, star time at Greenwich). For Local Sidereal Time, type your longitude (east positive, west negative). Your location is never requested.

Controls

Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom. Keys: ← → or 1–6 change scene, T tour, R replay, S sound (play/pause the orchestra), O orchestra panel, B Bartels strip, E enlarge or minimize the strip (or double-click its header), H hide text, I about.
